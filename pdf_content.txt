2. Executive Summary 
 
Problem Statement 
The COVID-19 pandemic created unprecedented psychosocial stressors for pregnant individuals 
worldwide. Elevated anxiety, depression, and fear of virus transmission posed significant risks to both 
maternal mental health and neonatal outcomes. Understanding how pandemic-related stress influenced 
birth outcomes is critical for informing public health preparedness and prenatal care policies. 
 
Approach 
This project presents an analysis of survey-based data from the Pregnancy during the COVID-19 
Pandemic (PdP) study conducted across Canada. The dataset comprised 10,773 records from 
pregnant individuals, capturing maternal demographics, mental health assessments (EPDS, PROMIS 
Anxiety), pandemic stress perceptions, and birth outcomes including birth weight, birth length, and 
NICU stay. 
 
Data was cleaned and transformed in Google Sheets, reducing the dataset to 5,389 high-quality 
records by removing incomplete entries and deriving additional analytical columns. Analysis included 
KPI development, exploratory data analysis, pivot-table-based segmentation, and interactive dashboard 
creation.

• Pandemic stress averaged 51.53/100, with perceived threat to the unborn baby being consistently 
the highest driver of stress scores. 
• Higher-income and higher-education groups showed measurably lower rates of severe postnatal

• Maternal age emerged as a significant predictor of preterm birth, with younger and older maternal

Key Recommendations 
• Targeted Mental Health Support: Implement screening and support programs specifically for 
expecting mothers, focusing on those with lower income or higher anxiety scores. 
• C-Section Awareness: Investigate the drivers of the elevated C-section rate and promote 
educational and support interventions for natural birth where clinically safe. 
• Resource Allocation: Direct additional healthcare resources toward vulnerable demographics 
identified in the analysis, particularly specific age groups and lower-income brackets.

3. Sector & Business Context 
 
Sector Overview 
The maternal and child health (MCH) sector is a vital component of public health. In Canada, 
approximately 350,000–380,000 births occur each year, supported by a wide network of prenatal, 
obstetric, neonatal, and mental health services. This sector accounts for substantial healthcare 
spending, with NICU care costing approximately CAD $2,000–$3,500 per infant per day. 
Source: NICU Cost Reference — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3203691/ 
 
Current Challenges 
• Mental Health Strain: Around 15–20% of pregnant individuals experience perinatal depression or 
anxiety, a burden worsened considerably by COVID-19. 
Source: Perinatal Mental Health Statistics — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-in-the-workplace 
• Health Inequities: Socioeconomic disparities continue to significantly affect maternal and neonatal 
outcomes across income and education strata. 
Source: Health Inequities in Maternal Care — https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/healthy-
living/perinatal-health-indicators-canada-2017.html 
• Data Gaps: Psychosocial stress data is rarely integrated into clinical care despite its proven 
impact on birth outcomes, representing a critical gap in standard prenatal protocols. 
Source: PdP Study — Psychosocial Data Integration — https://www.pdp-pdc.ca/en 
• Pandemic Readiness: Health systems were broadly underprepared for the indirect impacts of 
COVID-19 on pregnant populations, underscoring the need for proactive frameworks. 
Source: COVID-19 & Pregnancy Outcomes — https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(20)30065-
7/fulltext 
 
Why This Problem Was Chosen 
The link between pandemic-related stress and pregnancy outcomes remains underexplored in 
mainstream clinical literature. By examining how psychological stress affects outcomes such as 
preterm birth, low birth weight, and NICU admissions, this analysis offers actionable insights for 
policymakers, healthcare providers, and researchers focused on maternal care and long-term child 
development.

4. Problem Statement & Objectives 
 
Formal Problem Definition 
To analyze and quantify the impact of COVID-19-related psychosocial stressors on pregnant individuals 
and their birth outcomes, using survey-based data from the Pregnancy during the COVID-19 Pandemic 
(PdP) project, and to derive actionable insights for improving maternal and neonatal care during public 
health emergencies.
